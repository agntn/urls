## What it knows

Arquivo.pt archives the Portuguese web first, but don't let the name fool you: `nuxt.com` and `example.com` both come back with captures. Every row carries a timestamp, so `firstSeen`, `lastSeen`, `from` and `to` all work.

## How it asks

One request to `/wayback/cdx` with `matchType=domain`, `output=json` and `fields=url,timestamp`, so subdomains come along. The answer is NDJSON, one capture per line, streamed and parsed as it arrives.

## Traps

- Without a `limit` the stream answers 200 with an empty body and hangs, so the provider always sends `limit=10000`. An answer that fills all 10 000 rows may have been cut by the archive, and the page says so with `truncated`.
- `output=txt` is rejected, which is why this one speaks NDJSON while Wayback speaks text.
- 10 000 rows are captures, not URLs. A popular page captured a thousand times eats a thousand rows and gives you one URL.
